# Accounts Receivable and Order to Cash (By Example)

**Course ID**: `accounts-receivable-and-order-to-cash` · **Format**: By Example.

**Scope note**: Covers billing, collection, and receivable evidence; sales-system construction is excluded.

## Why this exists · the big idea

- **The problem before the solution**: invoicing can report revenue and receivables that are unlikely or impossible to collect.
- **Keep-this-if-you-forget-everything**: trace every receivable from agreed obligation through billing to cash or documented follow-up.

## Prerequisites

- **Prior courses**: `journal-entries-and-posting-mechanics`, `accrual-accounting-and-revenue-recognition`.
- **Assumed knowledge**: cutoff, evidence, and receivable basics.

## Accuracy notes

- [Verified — stable domain fact] Receivable controls reconcile billing, customer balances, and cash application.

## Concepts

- **co-01 · customer-order** — agreed commercial request.
- **co-02 · fulfillment-evidence** — proof of delivered performance.
- **co-03 · billing** — claim issued to the customer.
- **co-04 · receivable-aging** — grouping by collection delay.
- **co-05 · cash-application** — matching cash to a claim.
- **co-06 · credit-note** — controlled reduction of a claim.
- **co-07 · collection-followup** — documented escalation.
- **co-08 · silent-failure** — an old receivable may keep the ledger balanced while overstating collectible value.

## Worked examples

### Beginner

- **ex-01 · billed-delivery** — trace one delivery from fulfillment evidence to receivable. (co-02–co-04)

### Intermediate

- **ex-02 · partial-cash-application** — apply a partial customer payment without losing the remaining claim. (co-05)

### Advanced

- **ex-03 · silent-failure-aged-balance** — investigate an overdue balance that remains mechanically valid. (co-04, co-08)

## Applied synthesis (no build — A6)

Prepare a paper receivables review with aging, evidence exceptions, and collection actions; do not build an order-to-cash system.
