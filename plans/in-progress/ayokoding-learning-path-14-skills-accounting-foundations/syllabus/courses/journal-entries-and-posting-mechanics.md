# Journal Entries and Posting Mechanics (By Example)

**Course ID**: `journal-entries-and-posting-mechanics` · **Format**: By Example.

**Scope note**: Applies source evidence to journal entries and posting controls; recognition policy belongs to the next course.

## Why this exists · the big idea

- **The problem before the solution**: a balanced ledger can still contain an entry that never represented the event claimed.
- **Keep-this-if-you-forget-everything**: post only a reviewable entry with evidence, date, account, and explanation.

## Prerequisites

- **Prior courses**: `financial-statements-and-close-cycle`.
- **Assumed knowledge**: account normal balances and the close cycle.

## Accuracy notes

- [Verified — stable domain fact] Journal controls need evidence and an audit trail; scenarios are original.

## Concepts

- **co-01 · journal** — chronological entry record.
- **co-02 · posting** — transfer to account balances.
- **co-03 · effective-date** — period assignment for an event.
- **co-04 · narration** — concise business explanation.
- **co-05 · source-link** — evidence reference.
- **co-06 · approval** — independent review point.
- **co-07 · reversal** — controlled correction of a prior entry.
- **co-08 · silent-failure** — balanced entries can still carry the wrong date or account.

## Worked examples

### Beginner

- **ex-01 · supplier-invoice-entry** — draft a dated, evidenced payable entry. (co-01–co-05)

### Intermediate

- **ex-02 · review-queue** — identify entries requiring approval before posting. (co-06)

### Advanced

- **ex-03 · silent-failure-backdate** — explain why a backdated balanced entry misstates a period. (co-03, co-08)

## Applied synthesis (no build — A6)

Audit a paper journal packet, approve or reject entries, and document corrections; do not implement posting software.
