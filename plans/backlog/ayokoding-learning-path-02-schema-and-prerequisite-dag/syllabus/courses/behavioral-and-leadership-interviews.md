**Scope note**: the behavioral and leadership rounds — STAR-structured storytelling, the senior/staff
rounds (influence, conflict, ambiguity, cross-team leadership), and — central for this section's
primary persona — the **layoff / employment-gap narrative**. No runnable code (Annotated-concept,
no-code): prose, worked answer scenarios, and story-structure artifacts. Pairs with
`technical-communication` (the general craft) and closes Phase 1.

## Why this exists · the big idea

- **The problem before the solution**: technically excellent candidates lose offers in the behavioral
  round — rambling stories with no result, taking no personal ownership ("we" with no "I"), or freezing
  on the layoff/gap question and sounding defensive or bitter. The round decides fit, trust, and level,
  and it is coachable.
- **Keep-this-if-you-forget-everything**: every answer is a **short, specific, first-person story with a
  measurable result and a reflection** — Situation, Task, Action (yours), Result, and what you learned —
  and the layoff/gap is a matter-of-fact, forward-looking line, not an apology.
- **Big ideas touched**: `correctness-vs-pragmatism` (a concrete real story beats a polished
  hypothetical), `taming-state` (a prepared story bank keeps you composed under pressure rather than
  improvising raw).

## Prerequisites

- **Prior topics**: `technical-communication` (clear, structured expression); the
  reader's own real work history as raw material.
- **Tools & environment**: a written **story bank** (a document of prepared STAR stories); a timer for
  drills; no code environment.
- **Assumed knowledge**: none technical — the material is communication and reflection; the primary
  persona brings real senior experience to draw stories from.

## Concepts

1. **co-01 · what-behavioral-rounds-score** — fit, self-awareness, ownership, collaboration, and level
   — inferred from how you tell real stories.
2. **co-02 · star-structure** — Situation, Task, Action, Result gives a story a legible arc the
   interviewer can follow and score.
3. **co-03 · reflection-extension** — closing with what you learned or would do differently signals
   growth and self-awareness.
4. **co-04 · first-person-ownership** — describing _your_ specific actions ("I") rather than the team's
   ("we") is what the round actually scores.
5. **co-05 · specificity-over-generality** — concrete details, names, numbers, and stakes make a story
   credible; vague generalities read as fabricated.
6. **co-06 · measurable-result** — quantifying the outcome (or its concrete effect where numbers do not
   exist) closes the story with evidence.
7. **co-07 · story-bank-preparation** — a prepared set of stories mapped to common themes (conflict,
   failure, leadership, impact) prevents freezing and repetition.
8. **co-08 · theme-mapping** — matching one prepared story to several possible questions maximizes
   coverage without memorizing dozens of answers.
9. **co-09 · conciseness-and-time** — a two-to-three-minute answer respects the round; rambling buries
   the signal and eats the time.
10. **co-10 · handling-failure-questions** — owning a real failure, its cause, and the lesson — without
    blame-shifting — is a strong, common signal.
11. **co-11 · conflict-and-disagreement** — describing a real disagreement resolved with respect and a
    good outcome demonstrates collaboration maturity.
12. **co-12 · leadership-without-authority** — showing influence, mentoring, or driving a decision
    without a title is the core senior/staff signal.
13. **co-13 · ambiguity-and-judgment** — narrating a decision made with incomplete information, and how
    you reduced the ambiguity, demonstrates senior judgment.
14. **co-14 · cross-team-influence** — aligning multiple teams or stakeholders behind a direction is a
    staff-level dimension.
15. **co-15 · layoff-narrative** — framing a layoff factually and without bitterness (it was a business
    decision, here is what I did with the time, here is why I am excited about this role) keeps the
    conversation forward-looking.
16. **co-16 · employment-gap-narrative** — explaining a gap (sabbatical, caregiving, health, learning)
    honestly and briefly, then pivoting to readiness, removes it as a concern.
17. **co-17 · reframing-a-setback** — presenting a career setback as a source of growth or clarity,
    truthfully, turns a liability into a maturity signal.
18. **co-18 · questions-for-the-interviewer** — thoughtful questions at the end signal genuine interest
    and evaluate mutual fit.
19. **co-19 · authenticity-and-honesty** — real, imperfect stories outperform polished fabrications;
    interviewers probe for the seams, and honesty survives the probe.
20. **co-20 · reading-the-level-bar** — calibrating story scope to the target level (IC vs senior vs
    staff) so the impact matches expectations.
21. **co-21 · composure-under-probing** — staying calm and specific when the interviewer digs into a
    story's details signals confidence and truthfulness.
22. **co-22 · values-and-motivation-alignment** — connecting your motivation and working style to the
    role and team, credibly, addresses the "why here" the round always asks.

## Tensions & trade-offs — when NOT to reach for this

- **Polish vs authenticity**: an over-rehearsed, word-perfect story reads as canned and invites
  skepticism; a slightly rough but genuine story with real detail scores higher. Prepare the structure,
  not the script.
- **Humility vs ownership**: over-crediting the team erases your contribution; over-claiming erases the
  team and reads as arrogant. The calibrated answer owns your specific part _and_ credits the collaboration.
- **When NOT to volunteer**: leading with the layoff or gap unprompted over-weights it — address it
  cleanly when asked, then move forward; do not make it the theme of the conversation.

## Lineage — why it beat the alternative

- Structured behavioral interviewing replaced gut-feel "culture fit" chats because unstructured
  behavioral judgments are noisy and bias-prone; asking for _specific past behavior_ against a
  consistent rubric produces a fairer, more predictive signal (past behavior predicts future behavior
  better than hypotheticals). STAR became the lingua franca because it makes a story gradable. For this
  section's laid-off / returning persona, the layoff and gap narratives are first-class content, not an
  afterthought — mishandled, they sink an otherwise strong loop; handled well, they are a non-issue.
  This module draws on `technical-communication` and closes Phase 1 into the
  [mock loop capstone](./capstone-interview-loop.md).

## Worked scenarios

No runnable code (Annotated-concept, no-code). Colocated under
`behavioral-and-leadership-interviews/learning/artifacts/` as prepared stories, scored answer
transcripts, and a story-bank template. Grouped by theme. Contiguous `ex-01..ex-42`. Every scenario
cites the `co-NN` it exercises.

### Theme A · STAR fundamentals & the story bank (ex 01–12)

1. **ex-01 · anatomy-of-a-star-answer** — annotate a strong answer, labeling S/T/A/R + reflection —
   verify all five parts are present and in order. (co-02, co-03)
2. **ex-02 · first-person-rewrite** — rewrite a "we"-heavy answer into owned first-person actions —
   verify the actions are attributable to the speaker. (co-04)
3. **ex-03 · add-specificity** — take a vague story and inject concrete detail, stakes, and a number —
   verify the revision names a measurable result. (co-05, co-06)
4. **ex-04 · quantify-the-result** — attach a metric or concrete effect to a story's outcome — verify
   the result is evidence, not assertion. (co-06)
5. **ex-05 · trim-to-time** — cut a rambling answer to under three minutes without losing the arc —
   verify the trimmed version keeps S/T/A/R. (co-09)
6. **ex-06 · build-a-story-bank** — draft a story bank of 6–8 real stories tagged by theme — verify each
   tag maps to ≥ 1 common question. (co-07, co-08)
7. **ex-07 · one-story-many-questions** — map a single strong story to three different prompts — verify
   each mapping reframes the same story's angle. (co-08)
8. **ex-08 · reflection-that-lands** — add a genuine "what I learned" to a story — verify it names a
   changed behavior, not a platitude. (co-03)
9. **ex-09 · authenticity-check** — probe a polished story for fabricated seams, then make it honest —
   verify the honest version survives a follow-up. (co-19, co-21)
10. **ex-10 · calibrate-to-level** — scope one story for IC vs senior vs staff impact — verify the
    scope matches each level bar. (co-20)
11. **ex-11 · closing-questions** — draft three thoughtful questions for the interviewer — verify each
    evaluates real fit, not filler. (co-18)
12. **ex-12 · why-this-role** — a credible motivation-and-fit answer tied to the team — verify it
    connects your style to the role. (co-22)

### Theme B · Senior & leadership dimensions (ex 13–26)

1. **ex-13 · own-a-real-failure** — a failure story owning cause + lesson without blame-shifting —
   verify the speaker holds the responsibility. (co-10)
2. **ex-14 · disagreement-resolved-well** — a conflict story resolved with respect and a good outcome —
   verify both the disagreement and the resolution appear. (co-11)
3. **ex-15 · influence-without-authority** — a story driving a decision with no title — verify the
   influence mechanism (data, trust, framing) is explicit. (co-12)
4. **ex-16 · mentoring-impact** — a mentoring/growth story with a concrete outcome for the mentee —
   verify the mentee's change is named. (co-12)
5. **ex-17 · decision-under-ambiguity** — a story making a call with incomplete information — verify how
   the ambiguity was reduced is stated. (co-13)
6. **ex-18 · align-multiple-teams** — a staff-level cross-team alignment story — verify multiple
   stakeholders and the alignment mechanism appear. (co-14)
7. **ex-19 · disagree-and-commit** — a story of committing to a decision you argued against — verify the
   commitment and its result. (co-11, co-12)
8. **ex-20 · drive-ambiguous-scope** — a story taking an under-defined problem to a shipped outcome —
   verify the scoping actions are owned. (co-13, co-04)
9. **ex-21 · handle-a-difficult-stakeholder** — a story managing a demanding stakeholder to a good
   outcome — verify respect + result. (co-11, co-14)
10. **ex-22 · cross-functional-tradeoff** — a story weighing eng vs product/design and landing a
    decision — verify both sides and the rationale. (co-13, co-16-adjacent)
11. **ex-23 · staff-level-impact-story** — a story with organization-wide or multi-quarter impact —
    verify the scope reads at staff altitude. (co-20, co-14)
12. **ex-24 · composure-under-probing** — a transcript where the interviewer digs and the candidate
    stays calm and specific — verify the details hold up. (co-21, co-19)
13. **ex-25 · learn-from-being-wrong** — a story where the speaker was wrong and changed course — verify
    the reflection is genuine. (co-03, co-10)
14. **ex-26 · self-score-a-behavioral-answer** — grade an answer against a rubric (structure, ownership,
    specificity, result, reflection) — verify every axis is rated. (co-01, co-02)

### Theme C · Layoff, gap & setback narratives (ex 27–36)

1. **ex-27 · layoff-one-liner** — a factual, non-bitter layoff line (business decision → what you did →
   why this role) — verify it is forward-looking and under 30 seconds. (co-15)
2. **ex-28 · layoff-follow-up** — handle a probing follow-up on the layoff calmly — verify no blame or
   defensiveness. (co-15, co-21)
3. **ex-29 · what-i-did-with-the-time** — frame the post-layoff period productively (learning, projects,
   this curriculum) — verify concrete, honest activity. (co-15, co-17)
4. **ex-30 · employment-gap-explanation** — a brief honest gap explanation (sabbatical/caregiving/
   health/learning) pivoting to readiness — verify the pivot is clean. (co-16)
5. **ex-31 · gap-without-oversharing** — explain a personal gap with appropriate boundaries — verify it
   is honest without over-disclosing. (co-16, co-19)
6. **ex-32 · reframe-a-setback-as-growth** — present a career setback as a source of clarity, truthfully
   — verify the growth is real, not spun. (co-17)
7. **ex-33 · returning-after-a-long-break** — address rustiness proactively with evidence of
   re-grounding (e.g. this section's work) — verify the evidence is concrete. (co-16, co-15)
8. **ex-34 · career-pivot-narrative** — explain a deliberate change of direction credibly — verify the
   motivation aligns with the target role. (co-22, co-17)
9. **ex-35 · dont-lead-with-it** — a transcript that addresses the layoff only when asked, then moves on
   — verify it is not over-weighted. (co-15, co-09)
10. **ex-36 · confidence-after-rejection** — reset composure and narrative after a prior loop rejection —
    verify the framing stays constructive. (co-17, co-21)

### Theme D · Full mock rounds (ex 37–42)

1. **ex-37 · full-behavioral-round** — a complete self-run behavioral round across 5 themes with rubric

## Capstone spec — intra-topic (concept → design/decision artifact)

- **Goal**: build a complete **story bank** of real STAR stories mapped to the common behavioral and
  leadership themes, including a rehearsed **layoff/gap narrative**, then run a self-administered mock
  behavioral round scored against a rubric.
- **Concepts exercised**: [ ] STAR + reflection + first-person ownership (co-02–co-04) [ ] specificity +
  measurable result (co-05, co-06) [ ] story bank + theme mapping (co-07, co-08) [ ] failure, conflict,
  leadership, ambiguity stories (co-10–co-13) [ ] layoff + gap + setback narratives (co-15–co-17)
  [ ] level calibration + composure (co-20, co-21).
- **Ordered steps**:
  1. `behavioral-and-leadership-interviews/learning/capstone/story-bank.md` — 8+ real STAR stories tagged
     by theme + a layoff/gap narrative. Verify each story is first-person with a measurable result.
  2. `behavioral-and-leadership-interviews/learning/capstone/mock-round.md` — a timed transcript
     answering ≥ 8 questions from the bank, including the layoff/gap probe. Verify each answer is
     STAR-structured and within time.
  3. `behavioral-and-leadership-interviews/learning/capstone/scoresheet.md` — self-score each answer on
     structure, ownership, specificity, result, and reflection. Verify every axis is rated.
- **Acceptance criteria**: the story bank covers all common themes with real first-person stories and a
  rehearsed layoff/gap narrative; the mock round answers are STAR-structured, on-time, and forward-
  looking; every answer is self-scored on every rubric axis.
- **Done bar**: produces the stated artifact (story bank + scored mock round) + web-verified.
