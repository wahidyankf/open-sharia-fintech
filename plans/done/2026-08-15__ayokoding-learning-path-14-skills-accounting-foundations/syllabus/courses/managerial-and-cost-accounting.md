# Managerial and Cost Accounting (By Example)

**Course ID**: `managerial-and-cost-accounting` · **Format**: By Example.

**Scope note**: Explains decision-useful cost assignment and variance interpretation; pricing-system construction is excluded.

## Why this exists · the big idea

- **The problem before the solution**: one plausible total cost can hide the activity that actually consumed resources.
- **Keep-this-if-you-forget-everything**: select a cost view for the decision, disclose its assumptions, and challenge surprising results.

## Prerequisites

- **Prior courses**: `financial-statements-and-close-cycle`.
- **Assumed knowledge**: account classes and period reporting.

## Accuracy notes

- [Verified — stable domain fact] Cost allocation choices influence decisions; scenarios are original and not standard illustrations.

## Concepts

- **co-01 · direct-cost** — traceable resource consumption.
- **co-02 · indirect-cost** — shared resource requiring assignment.
- **co-03 · cost-driver** — activity used to allocate a shared cost.
- **co-04 · fixed-cost** — cost insensitive to short-run volume.
- **co-05 · variable-cost** — cost linked to activity volume.
- **co-06 · contribution** — revenue less variable cost view.
- **co-07 · variance** — difference between expectation and result.
- **co-08 · silent-failure** — a total can foot while its allocation basis drives the wrong decision.

## Worked examples

### Beginner

- **ex-01 · traceable-material** — distinguish a direct input from shared supervision. (co-01, co-02)

### Intermediate

- **ex-02 · driver-choice** — compare two allocation drivers and state the decision consequence. (co-03–co-06)

### Advanced

- **ex-03 · silent-failure-average-cost** — identify a profitable-looking product made unprofitable by a hidden driver. (co-07, co-08)

## Applied synthesis (no build — A6)

Review a paper cost report, select a transparent driver, and write a decision memo; do not build a costing application.
