# Inventory and COGS Accounting (By Example)

**Course ID**: `inventory-and-cogs-accounting` · **Format**: By Example.

**Scope note**: Connects inventory evidence, movement, and cost of goods sold; warehouse-system implementation is excluded.

## Why this exists · the big idea

- **The problem before the solution**: a count can agree with a ledger while both reflect the wrong ownership or cost flow.
- **Keep-this-if-you-forget-everything**: reconcile physical existence, ownership, movement, and cost before trusting margin.

## Prerequisites

- **Prior courses**: `accounts-payable-and-procure-to-pay`, `managerial-and-cost-accounting`.
- **Assumed knowledge**: evidence matching and cost assignment.

## Accuracy notes

- [Web-cited: https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-2-inventories/; accessed 2026-08-15] Examples are original and do not reproduce standard illustrations.

## Concepts

- **co-01 · inventory-existence** — item is physically or contractually controlled.
- **co-02 · ownership-cutoff** — receipt and ownership timing.
- **co-03 · cost-flow** — consistent assignment of item costs.
- **co-04 · cogs** — released inventory cost matched to sale.
- **co-05 · count-variance** — difference between record and count.
- **co-06 · shrinkage** — unexplained inventory loss.
- **co-07 · net-realizable-value** — recoverability review concept.
- **co-08 · silent-failure** — a consistent count can still use an incorrect cost or ownership cutoff.

## Worked examples

### Beginner

- **ex-01 · receipt-to-stock** — trace a received item into inventory evidence. (co-01, co-02)

### Intermediate

- **ex-02 · release-to-cogs** — explain inventory reduction and cost of goods sold for a shipment. (co-03, co-04)

### Advanced

- **ex-03 · silent-failure-consigned-stock** — identify a balanced inventory overstatement caused by ownership confusion. (co-02, co-08)

## Applied synthesis (no build — A6)

Reconcile a paper stock count, movement report, and cost schedule; do not build an inventory system.
