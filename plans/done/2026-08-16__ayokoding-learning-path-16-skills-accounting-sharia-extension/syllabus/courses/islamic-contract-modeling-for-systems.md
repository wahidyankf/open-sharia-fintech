# Islamic Contract Modeling for Systems (By Example)

**Course ID**: `islamic-contract-modeling-for-systems` · **Scope note**: models documented contract facts; legal opinion and transaction execution are excluded.

## Why this exists

A murabaha can resemble a conventional amortising loan numerically while its trade, underlying asset, fixed disclosed markup, receivable, and revenue evidence must be modelled differently.

## Prerequisites

- **Prior courses**: `sharia-accounting-and-aaoifi-standards`, `chart-of-accounts-and-data-modeling`.

## Accuracy notes

- [Web-cited: https://aaoifi.com/financial-accounting-standards/?lang=en; accessed 2026-08-16] Contract scenarios are original and do not state legal rulings.

## Concepts

- **co-01 · murabaha** — disclosed-markup trade model.
- **co-02 · underlying-asset** — identified trade subject.
- **co-03 · point-of-sale** — documented contract moment.
- **co-04 · fixed-markup** — disclosed trade amount.
- **co-05 · receivable** — amount due from sale.
- **co-06 · sale-revenue** — trade recognition view.
- **co-07 · amortising-loan** — contrasting conventional model.
- **co-08 · silent-failure** — a similar schedule can conceal the wrong economic model.

## Worked examples

- **ex-01 · asset-and-sale-facts** — record asset, price, markup, and sale evidence.
- **ex-02 · schedule-comparison** — compare a murabaha receivable with an amortising loan.
- **ex-03 · silent-failure-loan-label** — detect an unsupported interest-income classification.
