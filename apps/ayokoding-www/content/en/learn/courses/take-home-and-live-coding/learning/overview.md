Work through 75 compact examples in sequence. The first 25 make a take-home inspectable; the next 25 rehearse paired implementation; the final 25 put the reviewer’s eye, recovery loop, and self-score on both formats. Every row states an artifact, a falsifiable check, and the concept(s) it exercises.

- **Take-home examples** (`ex-01` through `ex-25`) turn a prompt into a deliberately small, reproducible, testable submission.
- **Live-coding examples** (`ex-26` through `ex-50`) make collaboration, clarification, narration, steering, and green increments observable.
- **Review and recovery examples** (`ex-51` through `ex-75`) rehearse debugging, trade-off defense, final review, and full-format practice.
- **Capstone** supplies one runnable take-home, one narrated live build, and a score sheet. Run the local checks from the indicated directories; they require no network or packages beyond `pytest`.

### Reading a worked example

An **artifact** is the concrete thing a reviewer or pair can inspect: a checklist, a README paragraph, a test, a command transcript, or a small code change. **Verify** is the narrowest observation that could falsify the claimed habit. The course values an explicit cut, a clear failure, and a safe rollback over an impressive but unreviewable expansion.
