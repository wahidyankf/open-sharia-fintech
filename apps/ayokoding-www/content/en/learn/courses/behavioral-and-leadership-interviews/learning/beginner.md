These first twelve worked scenarios make an answer short, evidence-backed, and reusable. Do not copy
the sample facts; replace them with facts you can defend in a follow-up.

### ex-01 · anatomy-of-a-star-answer

**Prompt**: “Tell me about a delivery you improved.” Maya says: “Our release train missed dates.” Her
task was to shorten review delay; she introduced a daily review rotation; median review wait fell from
three days to one; she now starts each project with an explicit owner. **Verify**: S/T/A/R and the
reflection are present in sequence. Exercises co-02, co-03.

### ex-02 · first-person-rewrite

Replace “we improved onboarding” with “I interviewed four new hires, wrote the first-week checklist,
and paired each hire with a rotating buddy; time to first merged change fell from ten to six days.”
**Verify**: the speaker’s actions are attributable. Exercises co-04.

### ex-03 · add-specificity

Vague: “I fixed a performance problem.” Specific: “At the Friday traffic peak, our checkout p95 was
2.4s; I profiled the N+1 product query and proposed batching, reducing p95 to 780ms.” **Verify**: the
story names stakes, detail, and evidence. Exercises co-05, co-06.

### ex-04 · quantify-the-result

When no percentage exists, say the concrete effect: “The support queue stopped receiving duplicate-
invoice reports after the change, and the finance lead signed off on the next close.” **Verify**: the
result is observable rather than an adjective. Exercises co-06.

### ex-05 · trim-to-time

Write four STAR bullets, rehearse for 150 seconds, then remove chronology that does not explain a
decision. **Verify**: the result and one action still fit under three minutes. Exercises co-09.

### ex-06 · build-a-story-bank

Create eight rows: failure, conflict, influence, mentoring, ambiguity, impact, layoff/gap, and
motivation. Add the prompt themes each can serve. **Verify**: every row maps to at least one prompt.
Exercises co-07, co-08.

### ex-07 · one-story-many-questions

The same migration can answer conflict (“how did I resolve sequencing?”), leadership (“how did I
align owners?”), or failure (“what did I misjudge?”). **Verify**: the facts stay stable while the
question-facing angle changes. Exercises co-08.

### ex-08 · reflection-that-lands

After a rushed rollout, say: “I now invite the on-call owner to the launch review before deciding the
date.” **Verify**: it names a behavior changed since the event. Exercises co-03.

### ex-09 · authenticity-check

Ask yourself “What did you try first?” and “Who disagreed?” If your answer loses detail, simplify it
until it is true. **Verify**: the same facts survive both probes. Exercises co-19, co-21.

### ex-10 · calibrate-to-level

An IC story emphasizes a sound component decision; a senior story describes a project outcome; a
staff/EM story describes durable multi-team leverage. **Verify**: no title is used as a substitute
for scope. Exercises co-20.

### ex-11 · closing-questions

Ask “Which engineering trade-off will this person own in the first six months?” and “How do teams
resolve cross-team priority conflicts?” **Verify**: each answer would affect your fit assessment.
Exercises co-18.

### ex-12 · why-this-role

“I enjoy making operational work visible. Your product’s reliability roadmap and the chance to pair
with product on it match the work I chose in my last role.” **Verify**: role evidence replaces vague
enthusiasm. Exercises co-22.
