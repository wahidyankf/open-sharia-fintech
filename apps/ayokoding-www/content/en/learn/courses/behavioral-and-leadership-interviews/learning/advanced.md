Career context deserves the same preparation as any other question. Tell the truth with appropriate
boundaries, then show present readiness. These scenarios are not instructions to disclose personal
information; they are practice in clear, forward-looking communication.

### ex-27 · layoff-one-liner

“My role was eliminated in a company-wide layoff. I used the following months to refresh my systems
work and contribute to an open-source maintenance project; I’m now looking for a role with this kind
of reliability ownership.” **Verify**: it is factual, forward-looking, and under 30 seconds. Exercises co-15.

### ex-28 · layoff-follow-up

“The selection was a business decision and I do not have more useful detail to add. What I can speak
to is how I kept my skills current and why this role fits.” **Verify**: no blame or defensiveness.
Exercises co-15, co-21.

### ex-29 · what-i-did-with-the-time

Name only true work: “I completed a systems-design study group, rebuilt my incident-response portfolio,
and met weekly with peers to review it.” **Verify**: activity is concrete and honest. Exercises co-15, co-17.

### ex-30 · employment-gap-explanation

“I took an employment gap to provide family care. That commitment has ended; I returned through a
structured refresh plan and can show the projects and practice that rebuilt my operating rhythm.”
**Verify**: the pivot is clean and no private diagnosis is needed. Exercises co-16.

### ex-31 · gap-without-oversharing

“I had a personal health matter that required time away. It is resolved enough for me to return, and
I am comfortable discussing the work I have done to prepare.” **Verify**: it is honest, bounded, and
does not invite unnecessary disclosure. Exercises co-16, co-19.

### ex-32 · reframe-a-setback-as-growth

“After a project was cancelled, I sought feedback on my stakeholder updates and now publish decision
notes before milestones.” **Verify**: the growth has an observable present practice. Exercises co-17.

### ex-33 · returning-after-a-long-break

For a re-entry conversation, name evidence: recent study, a reviewed portfolio, timed rehearsals, and
a current peer network. **Verify**: readiness is demonstrated rather than claimed. Exercises co-16, co-15.

### ex-34 · career-pivot-narrative

“I moved from support tooling to platform engineering after repeatedly seeing the same integration
failure; the target role lets me solve that root cause.” **Verify**: motivation connects to work.
Exercises co-22, co-17.

### ex-35 · dont-lead-with-it

Answer a project question with the project; address the layoff only if asked, then return to the
question’s purpose. **Verify**: career context is not made the interview’s theme. Exercises co-15, co-09.

### ex-36 · confidence-after-rejection

“That loop showed I needed a clearer impact story. I rebuilt the story bank, sought two mock rounds,
and can now explain my decisions with the result.” **Verify**: the framing stays constructive. Exercises co-17, co-21.

### ex-37 · full-behavioral-round

Run five prompts—impact, failure, conflict, ambiguity, motivation—then score every answer against
the five-axis rubric. **Verify**: each answer has STAR structure and a rating. Exercises co-01–co-10.

### ex-38 · senior-leadership-round

Run influence, conflict, ambiguity, and cross-team prompts at the target level. **Verify**: every
answer shows the intended senior, staff, or EM scope. Exercises co-11–co-14, co-20.

### ex-39 · layoff-and-gap-drill

Practice the layoff, employment gap, and re-entry prompts with one follow-up each; use a timer and a
peer who can stop vague answers. **Verify**: all responses are calm, forward-looking, and specific.
Exercises co-15–co-17, co-21.

### ex-40 · why-here-and-questions

Give a role-grounded motivation answer, then ask two questions that test mutual fit. **Verify**: both
halves connect to real work. Exercises co-22, co-18.

### ex-41 · mixed-technical-behavioral

Explain a technical decision, then answer “How did you bring others along?” **Verify**: the account
names your action, a concrete detail, and an influence mechanism. Exercises co-04, co-05, co-12.
