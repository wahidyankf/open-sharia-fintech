Practice aloud. First recall the rule, then apply it to a real story, then self-score it. Use a timer
and a peer for one of the longer drills; no code or runtime is involved.

## Recall

1. **co-01/co-02**: What makes a behavioral answer scorable? <details><summary>Answer</summary>A
   specific STAR story whose actions and outcome a listener can identify.</details>
2. **co-03/co-04**: What turns an answer from team history into your evidence? <details><summary>Answer</summary>First-person decisions plus a changed behavior.</details>
3. **co-05/co-06**: What makes a claim credible? <details><summary>Answer</summary>A concrete stake and measurable or observable result.</details>
4. **co-07/co-08**: Why keep a story bank? <details><summary>Answer</summary>It prevents freezing and lets one true event answer several themes.</details>
5. **co-09**: What is the time limit? <details><summary>Answer</summary>Usually two to three minutes, with room for follow-ups.</details>
6. **co-10/co-11**: How do failure and conflict stories earn trust? <details><summary>Answer</summary>Own the part you controlled, respect others, and name correction or resolution.</details>
7. **co-12–co-14**: What is senior/staff/EM evidence? <details><summary>Answer</summary>Influence, judgment under ambiguity, and cross-team alignment, even without formal authority.</details>
8. **co-15–co-17**: How should a layoff, employment gap, or re-entry answer sound? <details><summary>Answer</summary>Truthful, brief, bounded, forward-looking, and supported by current readiness.</details>
9. **co-18/co-22**: What do closing questions and “why here?” show? <details><summary>Answer</summary>Mutual-fit judgment grounded in the work.</details>
10. **co-19–co-21**: How do you handle probing? <details><summary>Answer</summary>Stay calm, be specific, and acknowledge what you do not know rather than inventing detail.</details>

## Applied drills

1. Record one 150-second STAR answer, listen once, and remove one vague sentence. Verify that all
   S/T/A/R parts and a reflection remain.
2. Ask a peer to interrupt twice with “What did _you_ do?” and “What changed?” Verify that your
   answer stays first person and evidence-backed.
3. Rehearse a senior/staff/EM leadership answer that includes influence without authority and one
   cross-team trade-off. Verify that scope matches the level you seek.
4. Rehearse a 30-second layoff or employment-gap answer only if it is part of your history; otherwise
   rehearse how you would respond with truthful boundaries. Verify that it is neither apologetic nor
   over-detailed.
5. Run a five-question mock, score structure, ownership, specificity, result, and reflection 1–5,
   then improve only the weakest dimension.
