## Goal

Produce a coherent, decision-ready GRC artifact set for the fictional Northstar Notes attachment
service. The set comprises a risk register, a control mapping, and a policy-plus-evidence plan. This
is a no-code governance capstone: do not create software, invent a certification claim, or treat a
framework as legal advice.

The capstone extends Worked Scenarios 3, 6, 12, 19, 21, and 30. It demonstrates the full chain:
risk → owner and treatment → control and framework outcome → operational evidence → assurance
decision.

## Concepts exercised

- [x] identify, assess, treat, and review risks (co-05 through co-08)
- [x] map controls to NIST CSF outcomes and risks in both directions (co-04, co-19)
- [x] distinguish policy, standard, procedure, and control type (co-16 through co-18)
- [x] make evidence attributable and reviewable (co-20)
- [x] roll evidence and exceptions into leadership assurance (co-25)

## Build order

1. Complete the [risk register](./risk-register). Every material risk needs a statement, inherent
   score, treatment, owner, residual score, and review point.
2. Complete the [control mapping](./control-mapping). Every control must trace to at least one risk,
   named NIST CSF outcome, owner, and evidence item; no control may be orphaned.
3. Complete the [policy and evidence plan](./policy-and-evidence). The policy states intent, the
   standard and procedure operationalize it, and the evidence plan says how performance is tested.
4. Review the three artifacts together. Confirm their owners, dates, and risk/control identifiers
   agree, then write the assurance decision named below.

## Acceptance criteria

- Each risk has an accountable owner, a treatment decision, and a date or event that triggers review.
- Each control maps backward to a risk and forward to a framework outcome and evidence record.
- The policy is enforceable; the standard is testable; the procedure has an operator; evidence has a
  control, period, source, reviewer, and exception result.
- The assurance summary names residual risks and a leadership decision rather than declaring the
  service “compliant” or “secure.”
- No assertion in the artifact set claims legal compliance, certification, or audit assurance.

## Done bar

A reviewer can follow any risk through its treatment, control, and evidence, then identify the
remaining exposure and the accountable decision maker without needing an oral explanation. That
traceability is the capstone's definition of a coherent GRC artifact set.
