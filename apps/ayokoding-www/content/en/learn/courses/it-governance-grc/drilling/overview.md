This drilling track follows the fixed sequence: recall, calculation, scenario judgment, design
exercise, then an automaticity checklist. Try each prompt before opening the answer. The artifacts
are fictional learning material; use qualified legal, audit, and business review for real decisions.

## Recall Q&A

**Q1 (co-01).** What is the difference between governance and management?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Governance evaluates options, directs priorities and risk tolerance, and monitors outcomes.
Management plans, builds, runs, and measures the work that carries out that direction.

</details>

**Q2 (co-07).** What fields make a risk-register entry decision-ready?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A clear risk statement, inherent rating, treatment, accountable owner, residual rating, due date or
review trigger, and current status. The owner and next review prevent the entry becoming an
unactionable observation.

</details>

**Q3 (co-08).** Name the four treatment choices and one required property of an acceptance.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Accept, mitigate, transfer, and avoid. An acceptance needs an accountable approver, a reason it is
within appetite, an expiry or review point, and any compensating controls.

</details>

**Q4 (co-19).** What does bidirectional control traceability mean?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Starting from a risk, a reviewer can reach its treatment, controls, and evidence. Starting from a
control, they can reach its owner, evidence, framework outcome, and the risks it reduces. Both
directions expose gaps and orphan controls.

</details>

**Q5 (co-20).** What makes evidence useful for assurance?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

It identifies the control, period, source, performer or reviewer, result, and exceptions. A file
without that context may be authentic but cannot reliably demonstrate that a control operated as
intended in the period under review.

</details>

**Q6 (co-21).** Distinguish RTO and RPO.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

RTO is the target time to restore a process after disruption. RPO is the maximum tolerable interval
of data loss. Both are business decisions that recovery testing must validate.

</details>

**Q7 (co-24).** What RACI rule prevents shared, invisible ownership?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Every activity has exactly one accountable role. Responsible, consulted, and informed roles can be
multiple, but they do not substitute for the person who owns the decision and outcome.

</details>

## Calculation practice

Northstar rates the likelihood of an attachment disclosure as 3 and its impact as 5 on its documented
five-point scale. It rates the residual likelihood 2 and residual impact 3 after treatment. What are
the inherent and residual scores, and what must still be recorded?

<details>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

The inherent score is 3 × 5 = 15; the residual score is 2 × 3 = 6. The arithmetic does not decide
whether 6 is acceptable. The register must still record the accountable owner, treatment, review
date or trigger, evidence expected, and the escalation path if the score is outside appetite.

</details>

## Scenario judgment

A GRC dashboard says “all green” because every scheduled access review was completed. The same
period has one unresolved service-account secret shared by three people and a restore test that
missed a critical target. What should the next report say?

<details>
<summary>Reasoned answer</summary>

Keep the completed access-review evidence, but report the shared secret and missed restore target as
material exceptions with their risk owners, ratings, remediation dates, and required leadership
decisions. A control-completion count is not a security conclusion. The report must describe the
period, evidence, residual risk, and decision request rather than compressing everything into green.

</details>

## Design exercise

Create a one-page GRC artifact set for a fictional customer-file export feature. Include:

1. Two risk statements with likelihood, impact, owner, treatment, residual score, and review point.
2. A control mapping that traces each risk to at least one NIST CSF outcome, owner, and evidence item.
3. A policy statement, one mandatory standard, one operating procedure, and one exception rule.
4. An assurance paragraph that names a residual risk and asks an accountable leader for a decision.

Review it using the observable rules from the learning track. Do not claim certification, legal
compliance, or perfect security.

## Automaticity checklist

- [ ] I can distinguish a business risk-tolerance decision from an operational implementation choice.
- [ ] I can write a risk statement with asset or objective, event, consequence, owner, and treatment.
- [ ] I can score risk consistently and explain why a residual score still needs an owner.
- [ ] I can map a control in both directions: risk to evidence and evidence back to risk.
- [ ] I can separate policy, standard, procedure, guideline, and evidence.
- [ ] I can classify a control by both functional timing and implementation nature.
- [ ] I can use RTO and RPO as business recovery targets rather than infrastructure slogans.
- [ ] I can write an assurance roll-up that exposes exceptions and requests a decision.
